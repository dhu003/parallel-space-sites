    Questions buyers actually ask

    How much business can I buy with $100,000 down?

    With a 10% SBA down payment, $100,000 down caps the price at $1,000,000. But the price also has to leave you enough after loan payments. For a $150,000 income need at 10% over 10 years, the smallest deal that pays you is about $624,000 for home services and about $950,000 for HVAC. This page shows both ends of that window for every industry.

    What DSCR do SBA lenders want?

    1.25x. The business's SDE must be at least 1.25 times the annual loan payment. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 the test runs on historical earnings, so a forward-looking estimate is a benchmark to compare against, not a pass/fail verdict. A quality-of-earnings report is mandatory for purchase prices of $3M or more.

    Why does the same down payment buy a bigger restaurant than a car wash?

    Price is a multiple of SDE, and multiples differ by industry. A restaurant sells at about 2.15x SDE, a car wash at about 4.5x SDE. One dollar of earnings buys more restaurant than car wash — but the restaurant also earns a thinner margin, so the loan payment eats more of it. The comparison table below shows the trade-off for all 19 industries.

    Is this lender approval?

    No. This is an estimate from researched industry averages, not a lender decision. Real approval depends on the actual business's tax returns, your credit, the lender's underwriting, and a quality-of-earnings review where required. Verify every number with your lender before you make an offer.

    Where the numbers come from

    Industry multiples and margins — the same researched defaults as the main acquisition calculator (fetched from the live repo, Nov 12, 2026): revenue, EBITDA margin, and SDE multiple per preset. SDE margin is EBITDA margin with the owner's pay already inside it, except general contractor, where SDE runs ~23% of revenue including owner comp while EBITDA alone is 8% (BizBuySell). Self-storage is priced on NOI at a 6.5% cap rate (sector avg 6.59%), not a multiple.

    Loan rate 10% — SBA 7(a) typical: prime 7.00% + 3.0% max spread for loans over $350K (Fed H.15 / SBA, Oct 2026). Re-check after the Dec 8–9 FOMC meeting.

    DSCR 1.25x — SBA 7(a) test under SOP 50 10 8.1, effective Oct 1, 2026.

    Down payment 10% — SBA 7(a) minimum (5% from your own funds); 15% is a typical buyer contribution.

    Closing costs 2% — typical deal costs run 1–4% of price before financing fees (legal + diligence 1–3%).
